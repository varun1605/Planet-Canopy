1. Welcome & what this guide covers

Welcome — your website is live, and from this point onwards you are in charge of its content. This guide explains exactly how to:

  • Add a new safari tour to your homepage
  • Update prices, dates, or descriptions of existing tours
  • See and respond to customer enquiries the moment they arrive
  • Upload, crop, and manage photographs
  • Avoid the few things that could cause problems

You don't need any technical or coding skills. Everything happens through a friendly visual editor in your browser, similar to writing a Google Doc.

How long will this take to learn? Most owners are completely confident with the dashboard within 30–45 minutes. After that, you'll only need to refer back to this guide for occasional questions.

  1. Open the URL above. You'll see a sign-in screen.

5. Managing your safari tours (Journeys)

The Journey document type controls every safari shown on your homepage. Each Journey is one tour package (e.g., "Ranthambore National Park").

The fields you'll fill in

FieldRequired?What to enter
TitleYesThe park name, exactly as you want it displayed. Example: Jim Corbett National Park.
SlugYesThe URL-friendly version. Click Generate next to the field — it auto-creates from the Title. Don't edit by hand.
LocationYesState or region. Example: Uttarakhand.
DurationNoOptional. Example: 3 Days / 2 Nights.
AvailabilityNoWhen the park is open. Example: Available Year Round or Oct – Jun.
ImageYesThe hero photograph shown on the card. Drag-drop a photo, or click to upload. (See section 7 for image tips.)
Short descriptionYes1–2 sentences shown on the card. Example: "Experience the royal Bengal tiger in its natural habitat across India's oldest national park."
PriceNoHow you want pricing shown. Example: ₹24,999 or Starting from ₹18,000/person.
HighlightsNoBullet points shown on the card. Type each highlight, press Enter, type the next. Example: Tiger Safari, Bird Watching, Elephant Ride.
Display orderNoA number that controls the order on the homepage. Lower numbers appear first. Suggestion: use 10, 20, 30, 40 — that way you can squeeze a new entry in between (e.g., 25) without renumbering.

How to add a new Journey

  1. In the dashboard, left rail → click Journey.
  2. Top right of the middle column → click + Create.
  3. Fill in the fields. The required ones (marked with a red asterisk) must be completed for you to publish.
  4. For the image: drag a photo into the image field, or click the field and select Upload. After it uploads, click the image to Edit hotspot — drag the dot to the focal point of the photo (the animal's face, for example).
  5. Top right of the editor → click Publish.
  6. Wait about a minute, then refresh your website. The new Journey will appear in the homepage gallery.

How to edit an existing Journey

  1. Left rail → Journey.
  2. Middle column → click the park you want to edit.
  3. Make your changes — update the price, swap the image, change the description, whatever.
  4. Click Publish.
  5. Refresh your website after a minute. Your edits are live.
What happens between save and publish? As you type, your changes save automatically as a draft. Drafts are private — only visible to you in the dashboard. Once you click Publish, the change becomes the live version of that Journey, visible on the public website.

How to delete a Journey

  1. Open the Journey you want to remove.
  2. Top right of the editor → click the ⋯ menu.
  3. Choose Delete.
  4. Confirm in the dialog.
  5. The Journey vanishes from your homepage within a minute.
Don't worry about mistakes Deleted documents are recoverable from the Recovery section in the sidebar for about 30 days. If you delete by accident, go there and restore it.

How to reorder which Journey appears first

The Display order field on each Journey controls the homepage sequence. Lower numbers appear earlier.

Example: if you currently have:

JourneyDisplay order
Ranthambore10
Jim Corbett20
Kaziranga30

…and you want Kaziranga first, change its display order to 5 (or any number lower than 10). Click Publish. Within a minute the homepage reorders.

6. Handling customer enquiries

How enquiries arrive

When a customer fills the booking form on your website, two things happen automatically:

  1. An email lands in theplanetcanopy1@gmail.com within seconds, with the customer's full details.
  2. A new Enquiry document appears in your dashboard, where you can track the follow-up.

You don't create enquiries — they're created by your customers via the website. Your job is to respond to them and track their status.

The enquiry workflow

Every new enquiry starts with status NEW. As you follow up, you change the status:

StatusMeaningWhen to set it
NewJust received, no action taken yet.Default for fresh enquiries.
ContactedYou've reached out to the customer.After you call/email/WhatsApp them.
BookedThey've confirmed their safari.Once a booking is finalised.
ClosedNo booking — lost or unresponsive.If they don't respond after follow-ups, or decide not to book.

Step-by-step: handling a new enquiry

  1. You receive the email notification at theplanetcanopy1@gmail.com with the customer's details.
  2. Open your dashboard → left rail → Enquiry. The new entry is at the top of the list.
  3. Open it. Read their message.
  4. Contact the customer using the email or phone number listed (or simply hit Reply on the email — it goes straight to them).
  5. After contact, return to the enquiry → change Status from New to Contacted.
  6. In the Internal notes field, write what was discussed. Example: "Spoke to Priya 12 May. Group of 4, prefers premium tents. Following up Wednesday with quote."
  7. Click Publish to save your update.
  8. When the customer eventually books (or declines), update Status one more time to Booked or Closed and Publish.

Fields on an enquiry

FieldEditable?What it's for
Full nameYes (only to fix typos)The customer's name as they entered it.
EmailYes (only to fix typos)Their reply-to address.
PhoneYes (only to fix typos)For calls or WhatsApp.
Preferred parkNoWhat the customer selected.
Preferred dateNoWhat they want.

Don't delete enquiries — even closed ones Old enquiries are valuable. They show you which parks attract the most interest, what time of year people enquire, and patterns that help you market better. Use the Closed status instead of deleting.

The only exception: if an enquiry is clearly spam (gibberish, irrelevant content), you can delete it from the ⋯ menu.

7. Adding & cropping images

What types of images work

  • Format: JPEG (.jpg / .jpeg) or PNG. JPEG is preferred for photographs.
  • Size: anywhere from 1200 to 4000 pixels wide is ideal. Don't worry about exact dimensions — the system optimises automatically.
  • Aspect ratio: landscape (wider than tall) generally looks best for tour cards.
  • File size: anything under 10 MB uploads smoothly.

Uploading an image

  1. In any document with an image field (like a Journey), click the empty image area or drag a file directly onto it.
  2. If you click, you'll see options: Upload (your computer), Select (already-uploaded images in your library), or paste a URL.
  3. Wait a few seconds while it uploads.

Setting the focal point (the "hotspot")

Different parts of your website show your image at different sizes — small on phones, large on desktops, square on cards, wide on detail pages. The hotspot tells the system which part of the image is the most important so it never accidentally crops out the main subject.

  1. Click the uploaded image in the editor.
  2. Click Edit hotspot.
  3. Drag the round dot to the focal point — usually the animal's face or eyes for wildlife photos.
  4. Optionally: drag the corners of the rectangle to set the "safe area" that should always be visible.
  5. Click outside the image to confirm.
Why this matters Without a hotspot, the system defaults to the centre of the image. If your tiger is on the left side of the photo, on small screens (phones) the website might crop the image to show empty grass and miss the tiger. Set the hotspot once and forget.

Replacing or removing an image

  • To swap for a different image: click the image → top of the panel → Replace.
  • To remove entirely: click the image → Remove. (The file stays in the asset library — you can re-link it later if needed.)

8. Email notifications

What you'll receive and when

Every customer enquiry triggers an email to theplanetcanopy1@gmail.com within seconds of submission. The subject looks like:

New enquiry from [Customer Name] — [Preferred Park]

The email body shows everything the customer entered, formatted as a neat table:

  • Their name, email, phone
  • The park they're interested in
  • Their preferred dates and party size
  • Any message or special requests
  • Time the enquiry was submitted
Reply to the email and it goes straight to the customer The notification email's Reply-To is set to the customer's address. Just hit Reply in Gmail and your message goes to them directly. No copying email addresses around.

Where to look if an enquiry email doesn't arrive

  1. Spam folder in Gmail. The first few enquiries from a new sender sometimes land there. Mark one "Not spam" and Gmail learns.
  2. Sanity dashboard → Enquiry tab. Even if the email is delayed or misrouted, the document is always created here. You'll never lose an enquiry.

9. Things to be careful about

Avoid thisWhy
Bulk-deleting enquiriesYou lose your historical record of customer interest. Use status Closed instead.
Sharing your password or API tokensAnyone with these can edit, delete, or read all your customer data. Treat them like banking credentials.
Editing the slug field by handSlugs power URLs. Changing them breaks shared links. Always use the Generate button next to the field.
Marking the same image as required for multiple Journeys without setting hotspotsThe same photo will look different on different cards. Set a hotspot per usage.
Posting placeholder text and forgetting to fill it later"Lorem ipsum" or test content can ship to your live website by accident. Always fill real content before clicking Publish.
If you ever click Publish and something looks wrong on the website Don't panic. Open the document → click the clock icon (History) → find the previous good version → click Restore → click Publish. The website reverts within a minute. Every change you've ever made is recorded.

10. Troubleshooting

"My change isn't showing on the website"

  1. Did you click Publish? Saving as a draft doesn't update the website. Look at the editor's top-right — the button must be greyed out / "Published" after you click.
  2. Wait at least 1 full minute, then hard-refresh the website (Cmd+Shift+R on Mac, Ctrl+F5 on Windows).
  3. If still not visible after 2 minutes, check the document for any red validation errors — they prevent publish.

"I can't click Publish — it's greyed out"

  • One or more required fields are empty or invalid. Scroll up and look for red asterisks or red error messages next to fields. Fix them and Publish becomes clickable.

"The dashboard won't load / loading forever"

  • Try a different browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox).
  • Disable VPN or aggressive ad-blocker temporarily.
  • Sign out and back in.
  • If it persists, contact your developer.

"I uploaded an image and it looks rotated / sideways"

  • The system reads the photo's built-in rotation tag. If it's wrong (rare, usually iPhone exports), open the photo in Apple Photos or Google Photos, rotate it correctly, save, and re-upload.

"I deleted something by mistake"

  • Sidebar → Recovery → find the document → restore. Available for ~30 days after deletion.
